# Rejection — Speaker Commitment / Factivity Representation

Date: 2026-09-01  
Verdict: **KILL-NOVELTY**

## Natural question

Does a model distinguish a proposition the speaker is committed to from one merely presented as someone’s thought, report, or possibility — even when the embedded proposition itself is identical?

## Semantic aliases

- factive vs non-factive
- speaker commitment
- projection inference
- belief commitment
- asserted/entailed embedded content

## Why it looked promising

The object is simple and natural: `Alice knows that p` and `Alice thinks that p` contain the same `p`, but differ in what the speaker commits to. The lexical proposition can be matched, and a causal intervention could in principle test whether commitment status is a reusable internal variable.

## Decisive kill evidence

Pan & Bergen, **“Are explicit belief representations necessary? A comparison between Large Language Models and Bayesian probabilistic models”** (NAACL 2025 Long) directly studies projection/speaker-commitment inference, including factive `know` versus non-factive `think`, and explicitly frames the scientific issue in terms of whether **explicit belief representations** are necessary to explain the judgments.

That makes an internal `speaker commitment / factivity direction` project too close to the same scientific object. The remaining novelty would mostly be replacing behavioral/model comparison with causal MI, which fails N2.

Source: https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.572/

## Strongest-neighbor warning

Do not revive as:

- factive vs nonfactive hidden states;
- projection representations;
- `know` vs `think` activation patching;
- speaker-belief direction;
- commitment steering.

The conceptual axis is already explicit in the strongest neighbor.

## Death code

`F2 / N0-N2 — direct scientific-object ownership; behavior/theory -> MI mechanism is too narrow.`

## Resurrection condition

Only reconsider if the new object is demonstrably distinct from projection/speaker belief commitment (for example, a separate semantic-status axis with natural cross-cells) rather than a new intervention on the same factivity contrast.
