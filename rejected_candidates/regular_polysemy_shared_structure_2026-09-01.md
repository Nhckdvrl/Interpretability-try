# Regular polysemy: shared generative structure vs discrete contextual senses

Date: 2026-09-01  
Verdict: **KILL-NOVELTY**

## Semantic aliases

- regular polysemy shared sense transformation
- generative lexical rule vs enumerated senses
- shared polysemy geometry
- continuous vs discrete sense architecture
- causal sense analogy mechanism

## Natural question

Do systematic polysemous alternations arise from a reusable shared lexical-semantic transformation that can generate related senses across words, or from separately contextualized sense states without a reusable cross-word operation?

## Decisive kill evidence

The concept-level representational question is already substantially owned.

1. Li & Armstrong (Cognitive Science 2024), *Probing the Representational Structure of Regular Polysemy via Sense Analogy Questions*, explicitly asks whether regular polysemes share cross-word representational mappings and gives positive evidence using contextual BERT vectors and sense analogies.
2. Temerko et al. (CoNLL 2025), *A Continuous Approach to Metaphorically Motivated Regular Polysemy in Language Models*, directly studies systematic sense alternations, graded regularity, novel-sense acceptability, and Llama/BERT sensitivity.
3. Scott et al. (arXiv Aug. 2026), *Divergent large language model predictions from convergent representations in ambiguous word pairs*, analyzes homonym/polyseme representations layer-by-layer in Llama-3.2 and Qwen2.5 and includes activation patching/ablation causal evidence.

A causal test for a shared regular-polysemy transformation would therefore most naturally read as representational claim -> stronger causal verification, not a new scientific question. N2 delta width is insufficient.

## Nearest-neighbor warning

Do not resurrect by changing metaphor type, model, sense inventory, SAE/probe, or by replacing representational analogy with activation patching. The occupied object is the representational organization of related senses and systematic sense mappings.

## Resurrection condition

Only reopen if a genuinely different external lexical-semantic theory axis is identified whose question is not equivalent to shared/continuous sense structure, and whose natural cross-cells are already behaviorally established in modern open models.
