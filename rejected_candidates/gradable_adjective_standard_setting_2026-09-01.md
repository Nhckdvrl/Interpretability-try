## Natural question considered

When LLMs interpret gradable adjectives, do relative adjectives dynamically construct a standard from the comparison class/context while absolute adjectives rely primarily on lexical scale endpoints, or is the apparent distinction implemented by a common contextual computation?

## Why it looked paper-scale

The relative/absolute distinction and comparison-class dependence are mature formal-semantic objects. Relative adjectives such as `tall` have context-dependent standards, while endpoint-oriented absolute adjectives such as `full`, `empty`, or `open` are classically analyzed as having minimum/maximum scale standards. Recent human work continues to causally separate comparison-class effects from alternative salience, finding selective comparison-class modulation for relative scales.
