## Natural question considered

> When an irrelevant numerical anchor shifts an LLM's estimate, does the model semantically retrieve anchor-consistent information (selective accessibility), or does it begin from the anchor and insufficiently adjust away from it?

This is a mature human cognitive debate, but the current LLM mechanism landscape is already too occupied for a safe concept-level delta, and the sharper human-theory cross-cells are not frozen in an existing modern open-model substrate.

## Decisive kill evidence

Recent LLM work has moved beyond merely documenting anchoring:

- **ACL 2026 Short — `Anchoring Depends on Confidence and Post-Training in Language Models`** studies anchoring across Llama and Qwen base/instruction/distilled models and links effect strength to internal confidence / post-training changes.
- **2026 — `Localizing Anchoring Pathways in Language Models`** performs attribution-based pathway/circuit localization on 7B–8B Qwen and Llama base/instruct checkpoints, including transfer of low/high-anchor pathways and post-training pathway changes.
- Earlier 2025 work also explicitly markets an `existence, mechanism, mitigations` account of anchoring in LLMs.

The classic selective-accessibility vs insufficient-adjustment distinction could in principle be wider than generic circuit localization, but a legitimate test requires theory-diagnostic natural cells such as self-generated vs externally supplied anchors / semantic-accessibility manipulations. The modern open-model anchoring artifacts located in this audit do not already establish those sharper cross-cells. Creating them and running models to discover whether the theory-diagnostic phenotype exists would violate the current registration discipline.
