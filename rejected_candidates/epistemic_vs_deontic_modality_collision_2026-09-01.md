# Rejection — Epistemic vs Deontic Modality

Date: 2026-09-01  
Verdict: **KILL-NOVELTY**

## Natural question

Does a model distinguish `must` meaning **very likely/necessarily true** from `must` meaning **required by a rule or norm**?

## Semantic aliases

- epistemic vs deontic modality
- probability/necessity vs obligation
- descriptive vs normative modal meaning
- must-as-belief vs must-as-duty

## Why it looked promising

The same modal surface forms can express two sharply different natural meanings, giving attractive lexical controls and a simple one-sentence Route-C question.

## Decisive kill evidence

Ozeki et al., **“Normative Reasoning in Large Language Models: A Comparative Benchmark from Logical and Modal Perspectives”** (BlackboxNLP 2025) directly compares LLM reasoning with **normative/deontic modals** against **epistemic modals**, explicitly because the two domains share formal structure. The paper introduces a dataset spanning formal reasoning patterns in both domains and cognitive factors.

Source: https://aclanthology.org/2025.blackboxnlp-1.17/

Thus the exact conceptual contrast is already an LLM research object. Internal causal localization would not by itself create enough N2 width.

## Strongest-neighbor warning

Do not revive as modal-sense direction, `must` disambiguation circuit, epistemic/deontic steering, or descriptive/normative modal architecture.

## Death code

`F2 / N0-N2 — direct recent object ownership.`

## Resurrection condition

Only reconsider a modality distinction that is genuinely orthogonal to the epistemic/deontic comparison and independently paper-scale.
