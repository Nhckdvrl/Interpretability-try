# Sound symbolism: sublexical iconic computation vs distributional association

Date: 2026-09-01  
Verdict: **KILL-NOVELTY**

## Semantic aliases

- bouba-kiki mechanism in LLMs
- phonological iconicity vs lexical association
- sublexical sound-symbolic features
- orthographic sound symbolism
- phoneme-level iconicity representation

## Natural question

When a word sounds "round", "sharp", "small", or "large", does a language model compose that judgment from sublexical/phonological features, or mainly recover learned lexical/cultural associations?

The question is natural and benchmark-independent. Sound symbolism / iconicity is a mature psycholinguistic phenomenon.

## Decisive kill evidence

The concept-level space is already occupied by a sequence of increasingly direct work:

1. EMNLP 2024 Main, *With Ears to See and Eyes to Hear: Sound Symbolism Experiments with Multimodal Large Language Models*, directly asks whether text/vision models can recover sound symbolism through orthography and related abstract cues.
2. 2025 work extends sound-symbolic behavior to modern LLMs with hidden-state / cross-lingual analyses.
3. AAAI 2026, *Do Language Models Associate Sound with Meaning? A Multimodal Study of Sound Symbolism*, uses natural words + systematically constructed pseudo-words, orthographic/IPA/audio inputs, and phoneme-level attention to identify the phonetic cues supporting iconic meaning.

Therefore a new project framed as `sublexical phonological computation vs distributional association` would mainly strengthen prior representational/interpretability claims with another causal method. That is not a safe N2 concept-level delta under the current search goal.

## Nearest-neighbor warning

Do not resurrect by changing language, using only textual LLMs, swapping bouba-kiki for magnitude symbolism, or replacing attention analysis with SAE/probing/activation patching.

## Resurrection condition

Only reopen if a genuinely different natural axis is found that sound-symbolism work has not asked (for example, an independently motivated cross-phenomenon computation), and the new headline does not reduce to explaining how iconicity is encoded.
