# Rejection Record — Knowledge vs Justified True Belief / Gettier Status

**Date:** 2026-09-01  
**Verdict:** `KILL-NOVELTY`

## Natural question

Can a language model distinguish a case where someone genuinely **knows** a fact from a case where the person has a justified true belief that is correct only through epistemic luck (a Gettier case)?

## Semantic aliases

- knowledge vs justified true belief
- Gettier status
- epistemic luck vs knowledge
- true justified belief vs genuine knowing
- knowledge attribution

## Why it looked promising

The distinction is extremely natural and philosophically independent of benchmarks: truth, belief, and justification can all be present without knowledge. Human experimental philosophy provides mature vignette sets with knowledge, false-belief/ignorance, and Gettier conditions, making it appear suitable for a simple Route-C representational question rather than generic factual recall.

## Decisive kill evidence

The exact LLM scientific object is now explicitly occupied.

- **Matsuda, Takeshita, Inarimori & Shimomichi (forthcoming 2026), `Do LLMs Show Human-Like Order Effects in Gettier Cases? A Case Study in Machine Experimental Philosophy`, Oxford Studies in Experimental Philosophy Vol. 6** directly studies LLM responses to Gettier cases as an object in their own right. Public author/project descriptions report that some LLMs are robust to order effects in Gettier cases and frame the work as machine experimental philosophy.

- **Inarimori et al. (Erkenntnis 2026), `Philosophical Significance of Artificial Intuitions`** explicitly discusses intrinsic research on LLM responses to Gettier cases, including a “strong” interpretation in which responses are taken to bear on underlying psychological states such as belief or knowledge.

- A current open research project, **`Belief, Knowledge, and Calibration: A Gettier Test for Language Models`**, further shows that the exact benchmark/object is active rather than untouched.

Thus the proposed concept-level question—whether an LLM treats justified-true-but-lucky cases as distinct from knowledge—is already a direct LLM experimental object. A hidden-state probe/activation-patching paper would primarily replace behavioral/X-Phi analysis with stronger MI rather than introduce a new scientific distinction.
