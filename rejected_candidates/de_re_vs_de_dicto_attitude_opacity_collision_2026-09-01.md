# Rejection Record — De Re vs De Dicto / Referential Opacity in Attitude Reports

**Date:** 2026-09-01  
**Verdict:** `KILL-NOVELTY`

## Natural question

Can a language model distinguish what is true **of an entity itself** from how that entity is represented inside another person's belief? For example, if Lois does not know that Superman is Clark Kent, can the model preserve the fact that `Superman = Clark Kent` while still treating `Lois believes Superman is strong` and `Lois believes Clark Kent is strong` differently?

## Semantic aliases

- de re vs de dicto
- referential opacity
- substitution under belief
- attitude-report opacity
- mode of presentation vs entity identity
- Superman / Clark Kent belief puzzle

## Why it looked promising

The distinction is a classic, benchmark-independent problem in philosophy of language and formal semantics. It naturally separates world-level identity from an attitude holder's representation of that entity, and therefore initially looked like a strong extension beyond ordinary factual entity knowledge.

## Decisive kill evidence

The exact neural-language-model scientific object is already directly occupied.

- **Kamath & Bradford, SCiL 2023, `Does a neural model understand the de re / de dicto distinction?`** explicitly confronts neural language models with intensional contexts producing de re/de dicto ambiguities and uses coreference behavior to test which interpretations the models support.
- **`Transparency Helps Reveal When Language Models Learn Meaning`, TACL 2023** explicitly formalizes referential opacity with examples such as `Lois Lane believes Superman is a hero` versus `Lois Lane believes Clark Kent is a hero`, emphasizing that co-denoting expressions yield different truth conditions inside belief contexts.
- The current 2026 formal-semantics literature continues to develop de re/de dicto and de re presupposition theories, so the external object is mature but not omitted from computational work.

Thus a Llama/Qwen hidden-state or activation-patching study of Clark-Kent/Superman substitution would primarily update an already direct neural-LM object with newer models and stronger MI. Under the current N2 rule, that is insufficient.
