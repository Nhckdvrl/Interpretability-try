# Independent N0 audit — Publicness–Coordination Dissociation

Date: 2026-08-29  
Role: independent collision audit, separate from the proposer-side shortlist record  
Lane: discovery

## Frozen claim

The proposed claim is LLM-specific: with the proposition, recipients, and first-order knowledge matched, a model recognizes the higher-order consequences of a public event and can use an explicitly stated common-knowledge bridge for coordination, but underuses natural publicness relative to separate private delivery.

## Search and full-text checks

Queries checked on 2026-08-29 included `LLM public announcement common knowledge coordination`, `large language model public private information coordination`, `dynamic epistemic logic LLM agents public announcement`, `common knowledge coordination language model`, `public private information coordination experiment`, and `higher-order knowledge coordination policy`.

The following sources were checked in full or at the available full-text level:

1. De Freitas, de Freitas, and colleagues, *Common knowledge, coordination, and strategic mentalizing in human social life* (2019), [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6628641/). This is a direct human public/shared/private information and coordination source, not an LLM study.
2. Bolander, Engelhardt, and Nicolet, *The Curse of Shared Knowledge: Recursive Belief Reasoning in a Coordination Game with Imperfect Information* (2020), [arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08849). It directly studies private/shared/common knowledge, recursive recognition, and consequential coordination, again in human participants.
3. Mullins and Symons, *Epistemic state updates in LLM agents via public announcement and graded modal logic* (2026), [Journal of Logic and Computation record](https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/exag018). This proposes an LLM-agent epistemic-state framework and public-announcement updates, but does not provide the frozen matched private/public natural-event plus explicit-CK downstream dissociation claimed here.
4. The Indiana University *Group coordination in humans and LLMs* project, [project page](https://cogai.luddy.indiana.edu/projects/coordination-gap-humans-llms.html), studies LLM coordination but uses a different group binary-search information structure and does not establish this publicness/common-knowledge contrast.

## Mother inclusion test

The human mother phenomenon is established: public/common knowledge can improve coordination relative to private or finite-order shared knowledge. That does not by itself collide with the proposed LLM-specific dissociation, because the frozen claim requires (i) matched first-order knowledge, (ii) recursive public-event recognition, (iii) an explicit-CK action bridge, and (iv) a selective failure of the natural public event at the policy interface. The sources checked above do not together provide that exact LLM package.

Result: `N0-PASS` conditional on a valid natural D0. This is not a priority claim and is not authorization.
